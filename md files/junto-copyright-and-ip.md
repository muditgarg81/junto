## 4. What actually matters (IP setup checklist)

1. **Pick the legal owner.** Decide whether copyright sits with you personally or a company you form. If you'll raise money or have cofounders, you'll want it in a company — set that up and assign the IP to it before it matters.
2. **Get assignment from every contributor.** Freelancers and contractors often own what they create unless they sign a written assignment to you. Cofounders too. Without this, you may not fully own your own app.
3. **Document the human authorship.** Junto was built largely with AI agents (Antigravity/Gemini). Purely AI-generated material with no human authorship has uncertain or no copyright protection in some jurisdictions (notably the US Copyright Office's current position; India is less settled). Your human direction, selection, editing, and arrangement are what anchor the claim — keep records that show meaningful human involvement.
4. **Add the notices above** (LICENSE/header/footer).
5. **Register if you want enforcement teeth (optional, per-version).** India: Registrar of Copyrights (prima facie evidence). US: Copyright Office (right to sue + statutory damages). Register major releases; you can't pre-register "future builds."
6. **Trademark the name and logo separately.** Copyright does **not** protect the brand name "Junto" — that's trademark, a different filing. And clear the name first: "Junto" is a common word and may already be claimed, so run a trademark search before you commit to it.

## 5. What copyright does and doesn't do

- **Protects:** your actual source code, UI designs, text, and graphics — the *expression*.
- **Does NOT protect:** the idea, the features, or how the app works. A competitor can legally build a similar trip-planning app with the same functionality; copyright only stops them copying your actual code and assets. Protecting functionality would require patents (hard and expensive for software), and protecting the brand requires trademark.

*Not legal advice. The ownership-entity choice, the AI-authorship question, registration, and the trademark search are each worth a short consult with an IP attorney (ideally one familiar with Indian IP law) before launch or fundraising.*
